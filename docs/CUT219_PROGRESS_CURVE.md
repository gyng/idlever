## Current state and evidence

`town::triggered` exposes Forge after the first positive returned Gold or
secured checkpoint. Kennel becomes eligible after a tame or owned companion.
Neither trigger requires Scout. The retained sixteen-seed Cut213 persistent
baseline exposes Forge after the first delve on 16/16 seeds and Kennel on 9/16;
Scout is hired on manual delve 3 on all sixteen. Depth after four eight-hour
absences is D8–D13. These measurements precede Cut216 and establish the opening
problem; they are not final-runtime balance acceptance.

Ordinary death now keeps the same hero and does not call `new_heir`. However,
`packages::temperament_open` still requires heir 3. `traits::arrived` requires
heir 3, ascension or a grave past D5; inherited traits require heir 5, and the
family lean requires heir 12. Cards are created at a wake and discarded at the
next send. The next-heir standing order is consumed by that wake. Successful
persistent heroes therefore cannot reach these choices naturally; dying does
not reliably repair the obsolete heir thresholds either.

Reusable kit selection already costs no Gold. The supply catalogue charges
zero for identified or craftable consumables, but offers leash unconditionally
before any Forge. Permanent Forge ranks remain paid and permanent; prepared
consumables remain limited by slots/quantities, not a recurring price. Preserve
these distinctions rather than reintroducing preparation fees.

Cut216's sixteen-seed earned collection survey reaches Body at 24–64 h, Hood
48–88 h and Boots 80–112 h in eight-hour check-in upper bounds. Its policy buys
affordable upgrades and offered packages. It does not measure the proposed
opening below. Preserve its exact runtime identity and measure staged changes
against a fresh matching public-action baseline before claiming pacing gains.

## Proposed stages

Use the existing per-resident `persistent.completed` counter, actual best depth,
Scout hire and positive real completed-exit Gold credits (including secured/protected death
credit). Bank, Return and Death all count
as completed attempts; no threshold requires death, heir replacement, deliberate
rebirth or a wall-clock login. Depth conditions prevent empty-return farming
from unlocking the later stages.

| Stage | Proposed authoritative gate | New decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | New town, manual House | Build House and delve |
| Guild | Existing Porter/Scout progression | Hire Scout within five manual delves |
| Forge | Scout hired, four completed delves, at least one actual positive completed-exit Gold credit | Manually build Forge; ranks remain paid |
| Leash | Forge manually built | Permanent free leash preparation unlock |
| Kennel | Scout hired and actual tamed/owned companion | Manually build Kennel; field a real pet |
| Temperament | Scout hired, six completed delves, best depth at least D5 | Choose an optional reusable temperament |
| Inherited trait | Eight completed delves, best depth at least D8 | Commit one lasting trait |
| Family lean | Twelve completed delves, best depth at least D13 | Commit a compatible WHEN change |

Recommended opening separates Forge at four attempts from temperament at six;
no forge upgrade purchase is required for Scout, temperament or other build
choices. A pre-Forge naturally tamed companion remains real and usable; the
Kennel gate must not remove it. Leash becomes eligible through actual manual
Forge construction, with truthful locked copy beforehand. No Gold usage fee,
extra claim action, new worker or obligatory leash click is added.

Built legacy plots retain availability. Unbuilt plots use the staged triggers,
even when their old milestones were previously met. Own equipment remains
usable before Forge construction; only buying new Forge ranks requires the
actual building. This avoids confiscating real finds or imposing rebuild chores.

## Easy builds and lasting commitments

Temperament is a build choice: once its stage is met, offer the four existing
validated packages through one stable Rust catalogue. Choosing, clearing and
reusing them is free. An opening must not silently equip a random personality.
A manual pick persists until changed; ordinary death, recovery, absence and
class changes do not replace it. Queue a change while away for next departure,
with actual versus queued choice shown. Preparation refits preserve actor,
route, RNG, paid items and rations. Literal rules and neutral counterfactuals
retain their existing exclusions.

The legacy `born` shape is currently the temperament's mechanical mapping. It
must remain the same build modifier and not masquerade as a lasting attribute.
The inherited trait is the commitment slot. Generate a stable three-card offer
once on reaching its survivor gate, using a tagged derived RNG independent of
combat RNG. The choice is optional; no automatic pick, death draw or repeated
read can rotate it. First choice is permanent until confirmed stat rebirth.
Removing/replacing that commitment requires the same preview/token/cooldown
boundary as the existing deliberate reset, not an ordinary recovery. Family
context is likewise optional and committed until that reset. It changes the
existing lasting trait's WHEN while preserving its gift, cost and tier; only
validated shipped combinations are available, each with its canonical formula. Existing chosen
traits/lean remain intact; no live actor changes mid-delve.

Retire user-facing next-heir policy. Replace its useful automation purpose with
an optional temperament order at actual departure: Keep selected by default;
Adapt chooses only unlocked temperament packages using the most recent actual
named threat; deterministic variety is opt-in. Record every actual change once
in the report. This order cannot change lasting traits, attributes or family
lean and cannot use unseen enemy facts. Never silently execute the old heir
policy through an ordinary recovery or rebirth. If this replacement cannot be
implemented in this cut, remove the unreachable order explicitly and leave
adaptive temperament automation open in the ledger; hiding it alone is not
acceptance.
